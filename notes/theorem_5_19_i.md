# Notes on `theorem_5_19_i`

## Informal statement (textbook style)

Let $P$ and $Q$ be partially ordered sets, with $P$ finite. Write $\mathcal{L}(P)$ and $\mathcal{L}(Q)$ for their lattices of **lower sets** (down-sets), ordered by inclusion, with top and bottom, meets, and joins as usual. Suppose

$$f : \mathcal{L}(P) \to \mathcal{L}(Q)$$

is a homomorphism of bounded lattices (it preserves $\top$, $\bot$, intersections, and unions of lower sets).

Then there is an **order-preserving** map $\varphi : Q \to P$ with the following property: for each $y \in Q$, $\varphi(y)$ is the **least** element $p \in P$ such that

$$y \in f\bigl(\mathord{\downarrow} p\bigr),$$

where $\mathord{\downarrow} p = \{x \in P : x \le p\}$ is the principal lower set generated by $p$.

---

## What it took to finish the proof

### Mathematical core

The statement asks for a monotone $\varphi : Q \to P$ such that $\varphi(y)$ is the *least* $p$ with $y \in f({\downarrow}p)$. That forces two layers:

1. **Nonemptiness** of $\{p \in P \mid y \in f(\mathrm{Iic}\,p)\}$ for every $y$.  
   Bounded lattice homomorphisms preserve finite sups and $\top$, so $f(\bigvee_p {\downarrow}p) = \bigvee_p f({\downarrow}p)$ and $\bigvee_p {\downarrow}p = \top$ on a finite $P$. Hence the union of the $f({\downarrow}p)$ is all of $Q$ as a lower set, so every $y$ lies in some $f({\downarrow}x)$.

2. **A true minimum in the partial order**, not just minimality under $\lt$.  
   The feasible sets are **directed downward** (two feasible points give a common lower bound still feasible), via $f$ preserving meets and the decomposition of a meet of principal lower sets into supers of principals on maximal points (`exists_le_of_mem_hom_Iic_inf`). On a **finite** poset, a downward-directed finite set has a unique least element; the proof realizes that by taking a **minimal** element of a finset of candidates (`Minimal` / `Finset.exists_minimal`) and then using directedness to show that minimal implies least.

So the real content is: *finite $P$* gives **global coverage** of $Q$ and **existence of a canonical minimal choice**; *homomorphism structure* gives **directedness**, turning “minimal” into “least.”

### Formalization cost

A large part of the effort was **alignment with Mathlib’s `LowerSet` API**, not the idea itself:

- `LowerSet` is ordered by **dual inclusion**, so `le_antisymm`, `coe_subset_coe`, and “$p \in \top$” needed careful orientation.
- `Finset.sup` on `LowerSet` is not the same proof obligation as `Finset.mem_sup` for `Finset`-valued maps; that motivated a dedicated `LowerSet.mem_finset_sup` lemma.
- Membership in meets of principal lower sets and the `map_finset_sup` / `map_top` path had to be spelled out so the informal “$y$ is in some $f({\downarrow}x)$” step checks in Lean.

After that, the **algorithm** is stable: candidates $\Rightarrow$ nonempty $\Rightarrow$ choose minimal $\Rightarrow$ compare with any other feasible point using the inf argument.

### Readability pass

Pulling out `LowerSet.univ_sup_Iic_eq_top`, `BoundedLatticeHom.univ_sup_Iic_map`, and `BoundedLatticeHom.exists_mem_Iic` moved long routine steps out of the theorem and made the main proof read as: *candidates finset → nonempty → minimal choice → `IsLeast` → monotonicity via lower sets.*

## Quality of the proof

### Strengths

- **Structure matches the mathematics:** coverage first, then canonical choice, then monotonicity from “$f({\downarrow}x)$ is a lower set.”
- **Reusable lemmas** in the aux section clarify the two main uses of homomorphism laws (sup + top vs. meet + local decomposition).
- **No axiom beyond standard Mathlib:** classical choice for `Classical.choose` on minimal elements and decidability instances are normal for this style of proof on `Finset`.

### Trade-offs / limitations

- **Heavy classical layer:** `Fintype`/`DecidableEq`, `open Classical`, and choice for $\varphi$ are not constructive; that is expected here but worth noting if you care about computability.
- **Minimality is proved via a finset encoding** (`filter` on `univ`) rather than abstractly on the set $\{p \mid y \in f({\downarrow}p)\}$; it is correct and convenient given `Fintype P` but ties the argument to that representation.
- **Specificity of the auxiliary lemmas:** some are very tailored to this file’s setup (`BoundedLatticeHom` + `LowerSet.Iic` + `Finset.univ`); they are not yet “textbook-level” standalone facts unless you later promote them or find Mathlib analogues.

### Overall

The proof is **mathematically sound and reasonably maintainable** after the refactor: the hard steps are localized, and the main theorem reads as a short narrative. The **difficulty was front-loaded** in understanding `LowerSet`’s order lattice and proving small membership/sup lemmas; the **conceptual depth** is moderate (finite + homomorphism + directed feasibility), not a single deep punchline.
